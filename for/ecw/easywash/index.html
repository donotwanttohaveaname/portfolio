01why this page

why a dedicated page comes before a new ecw.fi

I agree with you: this is a more focused project than the full redesign I proposed in October, and it pays back sooner. Here is how I'd put the case to management.

the gap
Two sites, no page for the buyer.

ecw.fi sells four businesses. easywash.com sells to drivers.

A category manager at a fuel retailer who hears about EasyWash has two places to go. One shows machines, chemicals, water treatment and a payment terminal on the same page. The other asks for a licence plate. Neither says what EasyWash does for an operator's revenue.

A dedicated page gives your outreach, your ads and your partners one link to send.

the competition
They each show one product.

Superoperator and Liquid Barcodes run full sites about the platform

Superoperator lists twenty named features, three price tiers and a four-step onboarding. Liquid Barcodes opens its car wash page with predictable revenue and names the machine makers and payment providers it connects to. A buyer with three tabs open learns more about them than about you.

The concept matches their level of detail and adds what their pages lack: a revenue calculator, a way in that keeps existing terminals, and owners quoting their own numbers.

the timing
The UI update needs a front door.

New product screens deserve a page built to show them

You have a big EasyWash UI update coming. The platform section in this concept has a screen for every part: app, plans, plate recognition, company account, dashboard. For now I drew those screens myself, with illustrative data, because the current app screenshots show customer brands you can't publish.

When the new UI is ready, the real screens go in and the page becomes launch material.

02what changed

your notes from october, applied

On our call you liked the layout, the segments and the message of the first concept, and corrected four things. All four are fixed here, and the page is now about EasyWash only.

The oil company names are gone. The numbers stay as "a Nordic fuel retailer, 200+ stations", with the name offered on a call. The day one logo is cleared, it goes into the proof band.
The camera reads the plate, everywhere on the page. The terminal takes the payment and runs the machine.
WashXL and Washman are shown as what they are: single sites. They now carry the "one site that sells around the clock" story.
Divi Vienā leads the cases: revenue tripled in 90 days, three to four hours to fit a station. For Poland and the Baltics it says more than any Nordic reference.
The terminal no longer has to be the way in. Variation A starts operators with the app and the camera, which is how you told me deals start.
Christ, Pineline and Clewer moved off the page. One footer link sends those buyers to ecw.fi.
Drivers get a way out. "For drivers" in the header goes to easywash.com, so the B2B page never has to explain the consumer app.
Every section is about EasyWash and what it earns an operator.

04what's on them

what both pages do, and why

The parts below are shared. The order differs: A goes problem, how it works, platform, where to start. B goes the bay, the hardware, then the platform behind it.

what's on the page
why
a question in the hero"How many wash sites do you run?" with five buttons, before any form.
A click is easier than a form, and the answer fills in the calculator. Superoperator and Liquid Barcodes both open with "Book a demo" and nothing else to press.
every section fits one screenOn a laptop, each scroll lands on one complete view.
A management meeting happens on a shared screen. Nothing is cut off halfway through a thought.
the big numbers in the first scroll100,000+ memberships, €3M a month, 700+ sites, revenue tripled in 90 days.
These close deals in your emails every week. Two are anonymised, one is public on easywash.com, one is Divi Vienā's.
seven platform parts, each with a screenSubscriptions, payments, plate recognition, fleet accounts, app and marketing, business intelligence, integrations.
This is your list. Each part has four facts from your decks and one screen, so a buyer can check a capability in ten seconds.
a revenue calculatorSites times members times price, with the fuel retailer's averages as reference points.
Neither competitor has one. It turns "100,000 subscriptions" into "what would that be on my five sites", which is the question a buyer takes to their boss.
three real cases with the owners' wordsDivi Vienā, Washman and WashXL, each with one number and one quote.
Small operators trust other small operators. Ronalds Vītols saying profit per wash went down and total profit went up is more convincing than anything I could write.
four doorsFuel retailers, wash chains, single sites, dealers and installers.
You liked the segments in October. Each door can become its own landing page for LinkedIn and outreach later.
a comparison without namesGeneric card terminal, app-only membership platform, EasyWash.
The "recognised by phone or by plate" row is your Aral argument in one line. Named pages such as "EasyWash vs Superoperator" can follow once you're comfortable with them.
a call booked in three stepsOne question, then contact details, then a calendar.
You told me a lead should be answered straight away, and that some weeks you can't. A calendar on the page books the call while the visitor is still interested.
