01why this page

why a dedicated page comes before a new ecw.fi

I agree with you: this is a more focused project than the full redesign I proposed in October, and it pays back sooner. Here is how I'd put the case to management.

the gap
Two sites, no page for the buyer.

ecw.fi sells four businesses. easywash.com sells to drivers.

A category manager at a fuel retailer who hears about EasyWash has two places to go. One shows machines, chemicals, water treatment and a payment terminal on the same page. The other asks for a licence plate. Neither says what EasyWash does for an operator's revenue.

A dedicated page gives your outreach, your ads and your partners one link to send.

the competition
They each show one product.

Superoperator and Liquid Barcodes run full sites about the platform

Superoperator lists twenty named features, three price tiers and a four-step onboarding. Liquid Barcodes opens its car wash page with predictable revenue and names the machine makers and payment providers it connects to. A buyer with three tabs open learns more about them than about you.

The concept matches their level of detail and adds what their pages lack: a revenue calculator, a way in that keeps existing terminals, and owners quoting their own numbers.

the timing
The UI update needs a front door.

New product screens deserve a page built to show them

You have a big EasyWash UI update coming. Every image in these concepts is your own: the terminal menu, the blue pillar, the camera, a terminal in the rain at Washman. I left out the current app screenshots, because they show customer brands you can't publish.

When the new UI is ready, its screens go straight in and the page becomes launch material.

Every section is about EasyWash and what it earns an operator.
