what i noticed before we've even started

From your public site only, without any access. The month one audit checks each of these against your own data, but they're worth knowing now.

structure

four product pages compete with blog posts for the same keyword

/360-degree-feedback-software/ and /blog/360-degree-feedback-software/ are both indexable, and so are the product and blog versions of employee survey tools, HR analytics tool and performance review software.

Google has to guess which page should rank, and often ranks neither well. These are four of your most valuable keywords.

technical

blog posts point Google to Finnish pages that don't exist

Each English post declares a Finnish and a Swedish version. In the 18 posts I sampled, every Finnish address returned a 404, and the Swedish ones I checked are the English post under a Swedish address.

Mixed language signals like this tend to get ignored or confuse which version ranks where. A quick fix.

money pages

your comparison pages exist, and nothing links to them

Culture Amp vs Teamspective, Leapsome vs Teamspective, Ennova vs Teamspective and the manufacturing page all sit under /lp/. Neither the homepage nor the alternatives page links to any of them.

Pages without internal links look unimportant to Google, and these are exactly the pages a buyer in final selection is looking for.

technical

small things that add up

Every URL in the sitemap carries the same last-modified date, the day of the latest build. Product page titles end in "| Teamspective | Teamspective". The ebook thank-you page is indexable and listed in the sitemap.

None of these is dramatic alone. Together they make it harder for Google and AI crawlers to see which pages changed and which ones matter.

the good news

you already have the hard part

There are comparison articles for Culture Amp, Lattice, Peakon, Qualtrics, Viva Glint and Ennova, several best-of lists for your product categories, and most of them were updated this spring. That's the content AI assistants pull their shortlists from.

So the first months are about making those pages better, connected and clearly yours, which is faster than starting from zero.

03the approach

why the plan starts with what you already have

1

fix before adding

With 119 English posts and two drops this year, the site needs to be clearer before it gets bigger. Every existing post gets a decision: keep, update, merge or redirect. Some of your best results in the first months will come from pages you already have.

2

build the pages buyers choose from

HR leaders comparing vendors search for alternatives, head-to-head comparisons and best-of lists. AI assistants build their shortlists from the same pages. That's where demo requests come from, so that's where the new work goes.

3

leads are the scoreboard

Impressions and traffic are early signals and we'll watch them. The number that decides whether a month was good is qualified leads who say they found you through Google or an AI assistant.

About the 10 to 20 pieces a month. You said more blog posts won't change anything, and I agree when they're more of the same. Most of those pieces are pages you already have, rewritten to answer the question better, with fresh facts, clearer structure and links to your product. The new ones are money pages: comparisons, alternatives, industry pages and integration pages. I draft with AI and edit every piece myself against a fixed quality bar, and I release them on a steady schedule. After two drops this year, a steady rhythm is safer than a burst.

month 1

Find out what already brings qualified leads, what's broken, and what's missing, before anything gets written.

  • SEO keyword plan. A money keyword map built from how HR buyers describe the problem, the industries you win in and the competitors that come up in sales calls.
  • Map every existing blog post. Each of the 119 English posts gets a target keyword and a decision: keep, update, merge or redirect.
  • Missing money keywords. The buyer searches that have no page on your site yet.
  • AEO audit. Around 50 real buyer questions asked in ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Copilot. Are you mentioned, cited, described correctly, and which sources and competitors show up instead?
  • Measurement. A "how did you hear about us?" field on the demo form if there isn't one, and the baselines we'll compare against.
